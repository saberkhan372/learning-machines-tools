# Learning Machines — Post-Camp Re-Engagement Plan (Canonical)

*Internal working doc — not a public site page. Outreach strategy, consent handling, and measurement; contains no participant PII. Supersedes `postcampclaude.md` and `postcampcodex.md`.*

**Status:** Working plan for re-engaging people who expressed interest but did not attend the live Summer 2026 camp, serving current participants after the camp, and building a responsible path toward an August showcase and a possible future cohort.

## Executive direction

Do not treat post-camp outreach as one large appeal. Build a **participation ladder** where every rung is useful on its own and each message has **one** primary call to action:

1. Try one five-minute activity.
2. Use one classroom-ready activity.
3. Observe or contribute to the August showcase.
4. Express interest in a future cohort.

The central invitation:

> You do not need to catch up, complete homework, use generative AI, or have attended the live sessions to enter Learning Machines now.

Lead with **participant-free, browser-based activities** — not recordings or named recaps. The goal is to give each person one obvious, low-pressure first move, not to persuade anyone into a large commitment immediately.

> **Final principle:** the invitation should not ask people to recover a missed experience. It should give them a new, complete experience small enough to begin now. Not "look at everything we made" — "try this one thing, notice what happens, and choose a next step only if it earns one."

## 1. What the engagement data supports

Use audited findings; do not turn directional patterns into causal claims.

**Verified pool snapshot**
- 81 responses; 81 unique email addresses.
- Experience: 55 regular users · 15 still figuring it out · 4 barely used · 3 unsure what counts · 4 actively choose not to use.
- Comfort: 28 excited · 26 curious but cautious · 17 mixed · 5 skeptical · 4 opposed-but-seeking-understanding · 1 blank.
- Most-selected interests: classroom activities 41 · bias/defaults 39 · creative work 38 · ethics 34 · image generation 33 · informed opinions 33 · video 24 · language-model mechanics 22 · under-the-hood mechanics 21.

**Supported planning signals**
- Many people expressed interest without an observed live-attendance signal.
- Classroom activities were the most frequently selected interest in the signup pool, but underrepresented among observed live attendees.
- Participants who said they were still figuring AI out had a higher observed-live rate in the reconciled data (7/15, 46.7%) than regular users (15/55, 27.3%). Treat that as directional evidence for preserving a beginner-accessible lane, not as proof that experience level caused attendance.
- The live room spanned enthusiastic, cautious, mixed, skeptical, and opposed-but-curious participants.
- Chat volume was one channel only, and highly concentrated.

**Claims the data does NOT establish**
- Why any particular person missed the sessions.
- That Saturday scheduling, summer travel, intimidation, or disinterest caused nonattendance.
- That classroom-interest respondents prefer async materials (hypothesis to test).
- That critical framing caused attendance.
- That the interest list is "mostly first-time AI users." Most matched respondents reported regular AI use, even though uncertain participants were an important, promising subgroup.

Use "you may not have been able to join," never an explanation of someone's absence on their behalf.

## 2. Consent & sharing boundary (non-negotiable)

The broad interest list is far larger than the live cohort. Outreach to it uses participant-free material until written-recap consent choices have been applied.

**Safe for broad outreach**
- Authored browser instruments and participant-free tool-detail pages
- The new participant-free Start Here page
- The three-line program argument
- The No-AI pathway
- Facilitator-created classroom activities
- Facilitator-created demo drawings, animations, screenshots, GIFs
- Project descriptions that don't identify or quote participants
- A showcase invitation that doesn't expose the cohort's identities or work

**Hold back from the broad list for now**
- Named session recaps; session recordings and their unlisted YouTube links
- Participant quotations, drawings, animations, prompts, projects, screenshots
- Session chat excerpts; guest/participant exchanges that identify people
- Any session page whose recap block, quotations, or recording links expose participant material

**Technical precision:** the recap pages use `noindex`, but they remain **accessible public webpages** — `noindex` discourages search indexing; it is not authentication, privacy, or access control. The recordings are **unlisted, not private.** Anyone with a link may view or reshare it. The reason to withhold these links is the current **consent scope**, not a claim that the URLs are technically group-only.

**Visual rule:** every campaign image/animation shows the interface alone, a facilitator-created demo artifact, or participant work with *separate* affirmative permission for this promotional use. Written-recap consent does not authorize promotional use of a participant's artifact or recording image.

## 3. Core message & accurate instrument descriptions

Conceptual spine:
- **Fluent text is not necessarily true.**
- **Plausible images are not necessarily neutral.**
- **Smooth video is not evidence that an event happened.**

Then the method:

> Perform one simplified constraint. Compare what happened. Make a claim the evidence can support. Name what the activity cannot establish.

**On "be the machine":** lead with the *concrete action* ("predict the next word," "draw a prompt in stages") — that is the accurate, load-bearing copy. "Be the machine" is the program's established shorthand and it pulls, so it may appear as a bounded flourish, but never as the literal claim that a person reproduces a model's architecture. Reconciled rule: concrete verb primary; "be the machine" only when immediately bounded.

**Accuracy quick-reference — say this, not that**

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| "Predict the next word" / "perform one simplified constraint the system faces" | "you become / are the neural network" |
| Human Diffusion Canvas: *draw a prompt in stages and notice which visual defaults you supply* (~10 min) | "draw a prompt out of noise" / "recreates diffusion" |
| Coherence Animator: *two short animations under different reference conditions, ~15–20 min* (five frames per run, nine unique drawings since the opening frame is shared) | "animate five frames, ~5 min" |
| "No-account simulations, plus a full No-AI pathway" | "every activity has a No-AI version" |
| "If I run another cohort…" *(until decided)* | "I'll run it again next summer" |
| "Much of the material is now available" | "it's all up now" |
| Most instruments are **authored teaching simulations**, not live models | implying the tools run live AI |

**Entry instruments (verbatim-ready):**
- **Next-Token Prediction Game** — *primary first rung.* Predict a continuation, compare human guesses with model probabilities, and ask whether the most probable answer has to be true. ~5 min solo.
- **Human Diffusion Canvas** — draw a prompt in stages and notice which conventions/defaults you supply when the language leaves details open. ~10 min. (Staged reveal is a teaching design, not how image models receive prompts.)
- **Coherence Animator** — make two short hand-drawn animations under different reference conditions and compare what persists across time. ~15–20 min. Models one coherence constraint; does not measure a video model's memory, context, compute, architecture, or understanding.

## 4. Deliverable A — PII-safe "Start Here After the Camp" page

**Promise:** *Start without catching up. Choose one activity based on the time and kind of participation you want.*

**Audience:** interested non-attendees; one-session attendees; educators seeking reusable material; artists, organizers, skeptics, curious visitors; people who want to observe without a generative-AI service.

**Structure**
1. **Five-minute entry** — one primary CTA: *Predict the next word · no account · ~5 min* → Next-Token Prediction Game.
2. **Prefer drawing?** — two secondary choices (Canvas ~10 min; Animator ~15–20 min), each with what it makes visible + one sentence on what it does not model.
3. **Teach it** — one *verified* classroom pack, not a resource catalog.
4. **No-AI route** — equal visual status; link the existing No-AI pathway.
5. **Join or observe** — after the date is locked: observe only / share one rough artifact or question / hear about a future cohort. Never require prior attendance.

**Exclusions:** no participant names, quotations, or artifacts; no recap/recording links; no embedded session video; no language implying a simulation is a live model; no promise of another cohort unless decided.

**Acceptance criteria:** one primary action visible without scrolling on a laptop · accurate time estimate per instrument · all core activities work without an account · No-AI route equal status · usable at 360/768/1280 px · keyboard nav + visible focus · no participant-data dependencies · external services disclosed · passes site QA with no console errors.

## 5. Deliverable B — one classroom-ready pack

Do not advertise it until it exists in the promised form.

**Recommended first pack:** *Fluent Is Not Necessarily True: A Next-Word Investigation* — runs in a browser, on paper, or as pure discussion.

**Required components:** audience/adaptation range · 15/30/60-min versions · learning objective · materials & setup · step-by-step protocol · exact prediction prompt · worked example · what it models · what it does **not** model · what evidence would verify a mechanism claim · discussion questions · No-AI version · access/privacy/age notes · printable Field Note · one bounded-claim sentence frame · authoritative sources.

**Shared student artifact:**
> I observed ___. The evidence is ___. This suggests ___, but it does not establish ___. My next test would be ___.

**Acceptance criteria:** teacher can run it without reading a recap and without an AI account · 30-min version fits its time in a dry run · mechanism *and* analogy limit both visible · printable version doesn't depend on color · no participant material · links + print styling verified before it appears in outreach.

## 6. Segmentation

Start with **two** versions; don't over-segment by inferred identity.

- **Segment A — educators / classroom-interest respondents.** Lead: *one classroom-ready activity you can run without student accounts.* Primary CTA: open the verified pack. Secondary: try the 5-min game as a learner first.
- **Segment B — general interest.** Lead: *try one five-minute investigation in your browser — no account, no catch-up.* Primary CTA: Next-Token Prediction Game. Secondary: drawing activity, No-AI pathway, future updates.

A form response is context, not a permanent identity — don't label people artist/skeptic/beginner unless they chose that pathway. Use voluntary self-selection: **reply with one word — explore · teach · showcase · observe · future.** Keep those replies private and use them only for the follow-up requested.

## 7. Outreach sequence

One primary CTA per message; the ladder unfolds across messages, never as five asks in email one. Use BCC or mail-merge (recipients never see each other's addresses) and an opt-out in every broad message.

- **Touch 1 — smallest useful action.** After the Start Here page is verified. Acknowledge they may not have joined, remove guilt, state no prior knowledge required, link one activity, invite one-word replies, include a quiet opt-out.
- **Touch 2 — classroom transfer.** 4–6 days later. Educator version → classroom-interest respondents (primary CTA: open the verified pack); general list → shorter note offering the pack as an optional route. Show one *facilitator-created* screenshot/excerpt, never participant work.
- **Touch 3 — dated showcase invitation.** After date/format locked, ~7–10 days out. State exact date/time/timezone/platform, whether it's recorded, whether names/work may appear after, share length, that polish and prior attendance are not required, and that camera-off/chat/observer participation is legitimate.
- **Final reminder.** ~48 h before. Short. No repeated countdowns.
- **Post-showcase follow-up.** Only to attendees/repliers/future-interest. Participant-free by default; apply event-specific consent before any names, quotations, screenshots, artifacts, or recording links.

## 11. Privacy-respecting measurement

**Measure:** one-word replies by pathway · aggregate Start Here visits · aggregate launches of the three instruments · classroom-pack opens · showcase interest / observer interest / confirmed contributors · future-cohort requests · qualitative replies about confusion/relevance/transfer.

**Do not infer:** a visit ≠ understanding · a Classroom membership ≠ recording use · a tool launch ≠ completion · silence ≠ disinterest · chat volume ≠ engagement.

**Practice:** aggregate GoatCounter only; no marketing pixels or cross-site tracking; keep reply segmentation private; BCC/mail-merge; opt-out in every broad message; document any external form/email service and honor its terms.

---

## 12. Success criteria

**Minimum useful:** Start Here makes one first step obvious · people enter without a recap/recording/account/prior knowledge · ≥1 classroom activity genuinely ready · some recipients self-identify a next pathway · the showcase has enough contributors or observers to justify its format · no participant material broadened beyond its consent scope.

**Stronger:** educators report using/adapting the pack · non-attendees complete a bounded observation through one instrument · new people join the showcase as observers or question-bringers · future-cohort interest is measurable without an application burden · feedback reveals which async route (tool / classroom pack / No-AI pathway / open studio / post-consent recording) people would actually use.

Success = meaningful small actions, not open rates.
