## 2. Consent form: creation, errors, and final model

### Apps Script errors explained

Two Google Apps Script errors were encountered.

#### “A consent form is already recorded”

The Apps Script project stored the IDs of the Form and tracker it had already created. Deleting the Form in Google Drive did not clear those saved script properties.

The builder therefore correctly refused to create another Form in the same recorded project and directed the user toward:

- `simplifyExistingRecapConsentForm()`; or
- `logRecapConsentLinks()`.

#### “The existing form IDs are not recorded in this Apps Script project”

`simplifyExistingRecapConsentForm()` can only work in the Apps Script project that created and recorded the original assets. A fresh Apps Script project has no stored Form or tracker IDs, even if a Form exists elsewhere in Drive.

The reliable paths are:

- use the original Apps Script project to simplify or log the existing assets; or
- use a fresh project and run `buildRecapConsentForm()` to create a new set.

### Successful build

The final run created:

- one participant-facing Google Form;
- one Form editor;
- one private consent-tracker spreadsheet.

The private editor and tracker URLs are intentionally omitted from this repository.

### Consent scope after manual revision

The final Form asks about:

1. name or alias;
2. inclusion in the written recaps;
3. use of specifically approved words in promotion or social media;
4. the exact promotional words the participant approves;
5. optional credit preferences, conditions, corrections, or session-specific choices.

The written-recap choices now distinguish:

- inclusion with name or alias;
- anonymous inclusion;
- removal of name, words, and described work from the public recaps.

The promotional choices now distinguish:

- approved words with name or alias;
- approved words anonymously;
- no promotional use.

### Recording boundary corrected

The earlier form and recap copy implied that participants could choose whether they appeared in the group recording. That was not operationally true because the recordings had already been shared as unlisted YouTube links.

The revised disclosure now says:

- recordings are handled separately from recap and promotional consent;
- the recordings are shared through unlisted YouTube links;
- anyone with a link may be able to view or reshare it;
- the recap-consent form controls public names, quotations, described work, and specifically approved promotional wording;

## 4. Removing the “never used to train AI” claim

The project previously made absolute claims such as:

- recordings are never used to train AI models;
- the recording is not used to train AI models;
- nothing here is ever used to train AI.

Those claims were too broad for recordings hosted on third-party platforms and were difficult to verify or maintain.

An intermediate revision narrowed the statement to actions attributed to Learning Machines. A final review concluded that even the narrower assurance was unnecessary and difficult to keep synchronized with the project's actual AI-assisted workflow.

The current site no longer uses an AI-training assurance as its privacy shorthand. It states the operational facts instead:

- recordings are shared separately by unlisted YouTube link;
- anyone with the link may be able to view or reshare them;
- unlisted does not mean private;
- written-recap consent controls public names, words, and described work, not access to or removal from an existing recording;
- Google Forms, YouTube, Zoom, and AI services process submitted material under their own account settings, retention policies, and terms;
- AI-assisted tools processed transcripts, captions, chat exports, and facilitator notes during recap drafting, followed by facilitator review against source records.

## 6. Permanent evidence-status standard

Created:

- `docs/evidence-status-standard.md`

The standard requires factual passages in future recaps and lessons to identify their status when ambiguity would matter.

The seven statuses are:

1. **Observed in the activity**
2. **Participant hypothesis**
3. **Facilitator interpretation**
4. **Guest's argument**
5. **Documented mechanism**
6. **Authored teaching simulation**
7. **Unknown or unverified**

The purpose is not to turn recaps into academic papers. It is to prevent a memorable observation, analogy, or guest position from quietly becoming a technical fact.

The standard includes guidance for:

- recap drafting;
- live lesson language;
- teaching analogies;
- quotations and attribution;
- technical sourcing;
- unresolved questions;
- final review before publication.

## 7. Older technical overclaims corrected

### Video systems

The older “Time Makes Failure Visible” materials described every video model as generating frames sequentially, having no memory of the whole clip, and drifting in a way that could never be fixed.

The corrected explanation says:

> Video systems must maintain relationships across time. Architectures use different temporal-conditioning strategies, and inconsistency can emerge gradually or abruptly.

This accommodates systems that process or denoise blocks across space and time rather than relying on one universal frame-by-frame architecture.

Updated areas include:

- the Time Makes Failure Visible concept bridge;
- the concept-bridge index;
- the Metronome Frame-Scrubber detail page;
- the authored Metronome simulation itself;
- Session 3 planning and facilitation references.

The Metronome tool is now clearly identified as an authored simulation. Its controls illustrate a teaching relationship; they are not evidence that widening a real model's “memory” automatically locks motion together.

### Language-model probability

The older Confidence Is Not Truth bridge described a high-probability token as the word that appeared most often in similar training contexts.

The corrected wording says:

> The model's learned parameters assign probabilities given the current context. Training frequency can influence those probabilities, but the result is not a direct corpus count or source check.

This distinguishes next-token training and learned parameters from direct phrase lookup at inference time.

### Base model versus product

Several bridges previously presented broad philosophical or product-level claims as universal facts, including:

- understanding requires continuity of self;
- a model cannot detect word salad;
- every system has one cutoff and no way to know current information;
- defaults are only decisions made by training-data assemblers.

The revised material distinguishes:

- base models from products;
- pretraining from fine-tuning;
- static model knowledge from browsing and retrieval;
- model context from interface memory features;
- learned patterns from safety and moderation layers;
- training data from tuning, filtering, product design, and sampling.

Philosophical interpretations are labeled as interpretations rather than mechanism claims.

### New structure

The page is now framed as **between-session fieldwork** with three primary routes:

#### Test

Inspect one frozen clip or create a new pair of human-drawn runs. Follow one feature, identify one exact change, separate observation from explanation, and name the practice-and-order confound.

#### Translate

Turn one investigation into a one-page classroom activity using drawing, a frozen example, or no AI. State what it makes visible, what it does not model, what evidence learners will record, and which human question matters.

#### Critique or refuse

Audit one confident claim such as “smooth video is evidence” or “nobody knows how AI works.” Label the passage's evidence status and name the source, corroboration, provenance, consent, or technical documentation still needed.

### Optional Studio extension

Building remains available without becoming the default expectation.

Suggested starting points come directly from the session:

- add onion-skin support so static details need less redrawing;
- explore timing and spacing in one subtle motion;
- sketch a “change one part of the frame” workflow;
- build a provenance activity;
- bring a question set rather than software;
- revise or deliberately refuse an activity.

Participants are invited to bring one rough thing the group can respond to. Slides and polish are not required.

### Shared evidence frame

Every route can use the same compact response:

> I examined ___. The clearest evidence is ___. It suggests ___, but it cannot establish ___. To check or use this responsibly, I would need ___. My next step is ___.

## 13. Long-term design direction

Created and expanded:

- `docs/learning-machines-long-term-design-update.md`

The plan keeps Learning Machines informal, hybrid, and interdisciplinary rather than turning it into a conventional credentialed curriculum.

### Identity

Keep **Learning Machines** and clarify it with:

> A field studio for how generative systems work, fail, and shape culture.

The four-line argument is:

- Fluent is not necessarily true.
- Plausible is not necessarily neutral.
- Smooth is not evidence.
- Possible is not appropriate.

### Recurring method

The long-term curriculum is the repeated inquiry loop:

1. Predict.
2. Perform or run the system.
3. Change one thing.
4. Compare evidence.
5. Make a bounded claim.
6. Name what the activity cannot prove.
7. Decide what a human should do next.

### Next-version direction

The plan recommends:

- one defining embodied centerpiece per session;
- no more than two supporting instruments in the live route;
- Teaching Cartoon Cards that state what maps, what does not, and what would verify the claim;
- assignments reframed as between-session fieldwork;
- recaps reframed as Field Reports;
- clearer simulation/model/provenance labels;
- Perform / Inspect / Question / Make / Teach doors on session pages;
- a smaller homepage and a deeper Instrument Cabinet;
- informal evidence of learning rather than quizzes or badges;
- a small interdisciplinary pilot before the next full cohort.
