Start here — no catching up

You don't need to have attended the live sessions, finish anything, or use an AI account. Pick one thing based on how much time you have. Most activities run in your browser, and there's a full route that uses no generative AI at all.

Fluent text is not necessarily true.

Plausible images are not necessarily neutral.

Smooth video is not evidence that an event happened.

The method: perform one simplified constraint the system faces, compare what happened, make a claim the evidence can support — and name what it can't.

§ Five minutes

the smallest way in
Recommended start · no account

Predict the next word

Guess how a sentence continues, then compare a spread of human guesses with an authored, illustrative probability distribution — and sit with a deceptively hard question: does the most probable answer have to be true?

What it isn't: live model output — the distribution here is an authored teaching approximation. And a likely word is never a checked fact.

§ Prefer drawing?

two hands-on routes
Images · ~10 min

Human Diffusion Canvas

Draw a prompt in stages and notice which visual defaults you supply when the words leave details open — the who, the setting, the lighting nobody asked for.

What it isn't: pulling an image out of noise. The staged reveal is a teaching design, not how an image model actually receives a prompt.

Motion · ~15–20 min

Coherence Animator

Make two short hand-drawn animations under different reference conditions and compare what stays consistent across time — and what drifts.

What it isn't: a video model. It performs one coherence constraint; it does not measure a real model's memory, compute, or architecture.
