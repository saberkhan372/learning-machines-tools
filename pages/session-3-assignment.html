§ A · Start from the live session

what the room actually investigated

We reviewed participant projects, compared two human-drawing reference conditions in the Coherence Animator, used animation craft to discuss timing and spacing, and heard Dr. Emily Thomforde argue that the “mystery” around AI is partly a question of what institutions and cultures decide to value.

The drawing comparison was useful, but it was not a controlled experiment: Run B came second, after more practice. Treat what you noticed as evidence about the activity—not as a measurement of a video model.

I examined ___. The clearest evidence is ___. It suggests ___, but it cannot establish ___. To check or use this responsibly, I would need ___. My next step is ___.

Inspect one sequence closely

Choose one frozen clip, or create a new pair of drawing runs in Coherence Animator. Follow one feature across time and mark the first exact change. Separate what you observed from why you think it happened. If you compare Run A and Run B, name the practice-and-order caveat instead of calling the result controlled.

§ C · Optional Studio extension

build from what participants noticed
Make · Build · Revise

Bring one rough thing the room can respond to

Make, adapt, question, or deliberately refuse something from any of the three sessions. Session 3 left several concrete starting points: add onion-skin support so static details need less redrawing; explore timing and spacing in one subtle motion; sketch a “change one part of the frame” workflow; build a provenance activity; or bring a set of questions instead of software.

Prepare a three-minute share: the thing itself, one piece of evidence behind it, the limit of your claim, and the question you want the group to help with. Rough work is welcome; slides are not required.

~20–60 min · any form · one thing you can point to

§ D · What to bring

evidence over polish

Bring a link, screenshot, short animation, frozen frame strip, worksheet row, lesson card, claim audit, sketch, or question set. It does not need to be polished or public.

Bring the evidence with it: an exact frame, timestamp, comparison, source, or documented mechanism. Be ready to say what it supports, what it cannot establish, and what you would investigate next.
